ABTA Appoints Rachel Moré-Oshodi, Infrastructure Investment CEO, to Advisory Board

Appointment Strengthens ABTA's Infrastructure Intelligence & Member Value

Rachel Moré-Oshodi, CEO of ARM-Harith Infrastructure Investment Ltd and driver of $7 billion+ in infrastructure projects, has joined the African Business Trade Association (ABTA) Advisory Board. Her appointment marks a significant expansion of ABTA's institutional expertise and deepens the organisation's capacity to deliver intelligence to its members.

With over two decades of experience mobilising capital for climate-resilient infrastructure across Africa, Rachel brings direct insight into the corridors, capital availability, and project readiness that shape African markets. Her strategic background, spanning the Inter-American Development Bank, International Finance Corporation, Rand Merchant Bank, and TotalEnergies, positions her to help deepen the calibre of research, briefings, and market analysis ABTA delivers.

"Rachel's appointment reflects our commitment to building advisory expertise that translates into member value," says Dr Dayo Abinusawa, Founder and Executive Director of ABTA. "Her understanding of African infrastructure ecosystems, institutional capital flows, and sustainable development will help deepen the intelligence we curate for members navigating Africa's most significant trade and investment corridors."

Rachel's expertise will shape ABTA's research agenda, webinar programming, and analytical briefings, ensuring members have access to insights informed by leaders who have already delivered significant outcomes at scale.

About Rachel Moré-Oshodi

As CEO of ARM-Harith Infrastructure Investment Ltd, Rachel mobilises capital for pioneering infrastructure across Africa's energy, transport, and ICT sectors. She has driven over $7 billion in award-winning projects and is a regular speaker at the World Economic Forum and UN General Assembly Climate Week. Rachel holds an MBA from Darden and executive education from Harvard Business School. She also founded Ruwah, a financial literacy platform advancing economic independence across Africa.

About ABTA

The African Business Trade Association (ABTA) connects serious investors, corporates, and institutions with data-driven intelligence on African trade and investment corridors. ABTA members access quarterly research, sector analysis, curated briefings, and membership in a network of institutional players committed to commercial outcomes across Africa. Learn more at www.abta.africa.

Two Decades of African Infrastructure Leadership

Rachel's career is a masterclass in combining global finance with African development impact. She started at AES Corporation developing energy assets, then progressed through the Inter-American Development Bank and International Finance Corporation, institutions where capital deployment decisions shape entire sectors. Her roles at Rand Merchant Bank and TotalEnergies demonstrated her ability to lead investments that merge financial rigour with sustainable outcomes.

What matters: Rachel has negotiated deals at an institutional scale, mobilised capital across borders, navigated regulatory complexity in emerging markets, and delivered outcomes that governments and development institutions recognise. She speaks regularly at the World Economic Forum and UN Climate Week. She holds an MBA from Darden and executive education from Harvard Business School.

This isn't theoretical expertise. This is institutional credibility earned over two decades of delivering significant outcomes.

Beyond Infrastructure: Building Sustainable African Markets

What distinguishes Rachel is that she doesn't optimise for extractive returns. She founded Ruwah, an innovative financial literacy platform designed to equip African youth with the skills necessary for financial independence. This reflects her conviction that Africa's sustainable development depends on creating self-sufficient, financially empowered populations.

For ABTA members pursuing long-term market entry strategies, this perspective matters. Rachel understands that sustainable African growth requires attention to infrastructure, governance, education, and financial inclusion, the systemic conditions that determine whether markets thrive or contract. That understanding now helps inform and deepen the research and analysis ABTA delivers to members.
